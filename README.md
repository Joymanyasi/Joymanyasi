# Joy Manyasi Kabaka

**Science Communicator | Genomics Storyteller | Founder, Sequence Africa**

I am a molecular biologist and science communicator based in Nairobi, Kenya. Through writing and research, I kept encountering the same gap. African biological data is being generated, interpreted, and governed using frameworks built for other realities. That observation became a research question. This profile documents the work that followed.

My current focus sits at the intersection of African genomics, AI governance, and scientific sovereignty. I examine what happens when reference systems, training datasets, and laboratory protocols calibrated elsewhere are applied to African populations, and what it would take to build better ones.

## Publications and Technical Work

**Under Review**
Beyond the White Leghorn: The Case for Breed-Specific Optimisation of Chicken Lung Organoids for Indigenous African Breeds. Avian Pathology (submitted June 2026). This short communication argues that the first chicken lung organoid system, built entirely around the White Leghorn, cannot address the most consequential questions in African avian influenza surveillance. It proposes the protocol adaptations needed to extend it to indigenous African breeds.

**Benchmarking Proposal (Terminal-Bench Science, Under Review)**
Benchmarking Reproducible Somatic Variant Interpretation Pipelines for Triple-Negative Breast Cancer in East African Populations. A framework defining the biological rules, control criteria, and validation standards needed to measure how accurately AI systems interpret clinical oncology variants in East African populations, populations that are largely absent from the datasets these systems were trained on.

**Peer-Reviewed Publication**
Expansions of chemosensory gene orthologs among selected tsetse fly species and their expressions in Glossina morsitans morsitans. PLOS Neglected Tropical Diseases (2020). Primary datasets deposited at NCBI SRA: PRJNA343267 and PRJNA343269.

## NGS Hub

The NGS Hub is built around scenario-based learning designed specifically for this reality. Rather than procedure manuals, it uses realistic research scenarios drawn from African settings to develop the critical judgment researchers need to recognise when standard tools are working against them and to know what to do next.
